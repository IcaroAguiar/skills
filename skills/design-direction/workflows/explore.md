# Explore

Use while the governing direction is genuinely undecided. Exploration is a
collaborative decision workflow, not a request for the user to design the
interface.

## 1. Calibrate intent

Ask at most four high-impact questions only when the available artifact and
brief do not already answer them. Resolve the desired posture, feeling,
presence, product qualities to preserve, references to approach or reject, and
acceptable expression risk. Prefer concrete contrasts such as precise versus
human or quiet versus present over generic style labels.

## 2. Lock the comparison surface

Use the exact surface that would change. Hold constant:

- task, requirements, content, and critical states;
- data and state values;
- viewport, device, platform, theme, and locale;
- primary journey and evidence boundary.

For a consequential or transactional surface, compare at least the initial,
post-primary-interaction, and blocking or consequential state. If rendering is
unavailable, use equivalent structured specifications or wireframes and mark
visual craft `N/V`.

## 3. Produce at most two directions

The candidates must differ in posture, governing relationship, composition,
attention path, type strategy, density, material roles, component strategy,
asset role, signature, and behavior—not only color or styling. Read
[precedent-library.md](../references/precedent-library.md) when precedent helps
generate or explain the alternatives.

For each candidate state:

```text
Posture:
Thesis:
Dominant composition:
Signature:
Contextual components:
Critical assets:
Platform behavior:
Strength:
Cost and risk:
Reason to reject:
```

Score both candidates with
[authorship-evaluation.md](../references/authorship-evaluation.md). Recommend
one and name what must not be combined between them.

## 4. Guide critique and freeze the decision

Ask which direction better represents the product and makes the task feel more
natural; identify what feels generic, excessive, artificial, trustworthy, or
worth preserving. Record the result with
[direction-decision.md](../templates/direction-decision.md), separating
approved, rejected, preserved, and unresolved attributes.

Stop before production changes. Continue to `execute` only after the user
approves a direction; use `refine` when the thesis is approved but material
details remain unresolved.

## Complete when

- comparison conditions are equivalent or their limits are explicit;
- the candidates differ in the governing dimensions named above;
- one candidate is recommended with its score, cost, risk, and rejection case;
- critique is recorded as approved, rejected, preserved, and unresolved;
- the direction is `awaiting approval`, `approved`, `rejected`, or routed to
  `refine`; production remains unchanged until approval.
