# Authorship Evaluation

Score only observable evidence. Use `PASS`, `FAIL`, and `N/V`; `N/V` is not a
pass and limits the highest supportable classification.

## Score anchors

- **0** — absent or harmful;
- **1** — generic or severely incoherent;
- **2** — partially intentional but below acceptable;
- **3** — coherent with material opportunities;
- **4** — strong, product-specific, and resilient;
- **5** — exceptional, precise, and fully evidenced.

## Axes

1. context specificity;
2. visible thesis;
3. dominant composition and attention path;
4. typography and rhythm;
5. color, material, form, and depth roles;
6. task-specific components and assets;
7. signature usefulness and restraint;
8. state and multi-screen coherence;
9. platform adaptation, motion, and feedback;
10. real-content resilience and evidence quality.

Record evidence beside every score. Do not calculate an approving average.
Apply the hard gates and these limiters:

- any fundamental `design-direction` failure returns the work to that skill;
- token-only changes fix the classification at `Styled`;
- reference costume caps context specificity and thesis at `2/5`;
- one static surface makes state, behavior, and system continuity `N/V`;
- untested runtime prevents `Refined` or `Premium` claims that depend on it;
- non-equivalent exploration prototypes prevent comparative recommendation;
- an explored direction without explicit user approval remains
  `awaiting approval`, not frozen for production execution.

## Before and after

When a trustworthy baseline exists, score the same axes under the same content,
state, viewport, theme, platform, and interaction conditions. Explain each
meaningful delta; a higher total cannot hide a new axis failure.

**Complete when:** every supported axis has a score and evidence, every `N/V`
limits the claim, and no average overrides a hard gate or limiter.
