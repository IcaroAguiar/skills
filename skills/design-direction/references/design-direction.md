# Authorial Design Direction

## Core rules

> Use surfaces for functional independence and sections for organization.
>
> Titles introduce content; they are not containers.
>
> Page titles establish identity; section titles establish structure.

Build hierarchy in this order:

1. content and language;
2. typography;
3. spacing and proximity;
4. alignment and grid;
5. dividers;
6. tonal change;
7. border;
8. elevation that communicates layer, focus, or interaction.

## Principles

- **Task-shaped:** derive structure from user tasks and information relationships before choosing components.
- **Surface budget:** spend visual boundaries only on independence, layer, state, selection, or action.
- **Proportional rhythm:** use proximity for relation and larger gaps for real level changes; scale controls and emphasis by frequency, importance, and risk.
- **Coherent grammar:** use one dominant compositional logic per page; vary it only when the relationship or mode changes.
- **Progressive complexity:** organize complexity by goal, dependency, priority, and moment of use instead of exposing or hiding everything uniformly.
- **Proportional emphasis:** reserve strongest contrast, size, color, and motion for the most consequential decision or state.
- **Contextual state:** place each state at the scope and object it describes; let actionable problems outrank passive confirmations.
- **Data-first operations:** make data dominate collections, states explain flow, and actions support safe transition.
- **System-integrated components:** make controls inherit the product's visual, behavioral, state, and accessibility contract regardless of their implementation source.
- **Domain specificity:** build identity from domain language, content, typography, rhythm, and composition.
- **Context fidelity:** keep the operational, analytical, transactional, editorial, or persuasive nature of the surface intact.
- **Platform-shaped mobile:** derive navigation, presentation, input, scroll, and adaptation from the platform and task topology rather than a narrowed desktop composition.
- **Authored mobile craft:** treat operational correctness as the floor; earn refinement through dominant composition, semantic rhythm, functional typography, differentiated materials, task-appropriate patterns, and causal feedback.

## Preferred patterns

- section-based layouts and proximity grouping;
- informative section headings and task-shaped PageHeaders;
- structured metadata lists;
- lists and tables for comparable collections;
- continuous side panels;
- one primary action per decision;
- coherent action groups by scope;
- native controls or library primitives deliberately integrated with the product system;
- tonal backgrounds for meaningful states;
- on-demand detail;
- progressive sections with summaries for long non-linear forms;
- empty states with meaning and a next action.

## Decision prompts

- What functional role justifies this frame?
- What page-level identity distinguishes this title from section headings?
- What relationship justifies this divider, tonal band, or split?
- What priority justifies this scale?
- Which fields are needed now, dependent, optional, or comparison-critical?
- What object, scope, action, and duration define this state?
- Can repeated actions move to selection, batch, context menu, or detail?
- Do these actions share object, frequency, consequence, reversibility, and priority?
- Does this control follow the same tokens, states, semantics, and interaction expectations as equivalent controls in the product?
- Is this collection for discovery or operational comparison?
- What domain-specific choice prevents the interface from becoming generic?
- Does this “premium” pattern match the surface mode, or only the reference?
- On mobile, what task topology, platform behavior, and adaptive presentation justify this composition?
- If this is called premium, what observable craft decision distinguishes it from a correct assembly of generic components?
