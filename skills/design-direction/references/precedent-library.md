# Precedent Library

Use precedent to study transferable relationships, not to choose a fashionable
skin. Verify the current source or inspect a user-provided artifact before
claiming that a named product uses a pattern.

## Source classes

### Platform and system standards

- Apple Human Interface Guidelines — platform behavior, layout, typography,
  accessibility, materials, and motion: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/
- Material Design 3 — adaptive composition, component behavior, color, type,
  and motion: https://m3.material.io/
- Carbon Design System — operational density, data display, structured lists,
  tables, and enterprise interaction: https://carbondesignsystem.com/

Treat these as standards or system precedents, not proof that one component is
correct for the destination product.

### Product precedents

- Linear — calm operational density, continuity, navigation, and motion. Start
  with first-party product-design writing:
  https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui
- Notion — content authorship, block relationships, direct manipulation, and
  progressive structure.
- ChatGPT and Claude — conversational hierarchy, streaming response, tools,
  citations, artifacts, and the relationship between prompt and result.

For Notion, ChatGPT, Claude, and other evolving products, inspect the current
interface or a dated capture. Do not encode their transient colors, spacing, or
component appearance as universal rules.

## Selection record

Use at most three relevant precedents. Include one closest functional precedent
and, when expression risk is high, one restrained counterexample.

```text
Source and date:
Artifact inspected:
Source class:
Relevant product and surface context:
Observed relationship:
Function:
Why it works there:
Dependency:
Transferable principle:
Translation for this product:
What not to copy:
Counterexample or sober fallback:
Evidence limit:
```

Prefer first-party design writing, system documentation, or directly inspected
runtime. Search-result thumbnails, moodboards without provenance, and generic
prompts such as `premium dashboard` are weak evidence.

**Complete when:** every used precedent has a verified artifact, functional
translation, dependency, non-copy boundary, and destination-specific result.
