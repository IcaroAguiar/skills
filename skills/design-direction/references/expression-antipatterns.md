# Expression Anti-Patterns

## Concept-only art direction

The idea exists in prose but does not materially change the render.

## Component-library composition

The result is a direct stack of available headers, cards, badges, and buttons.

## Style-token makeover

A claimed new art direction changes only color, typeface, radius, spacing, or
shadow without addressing the requested composition or product identity.
An explicitly requested token or spacing refinement within an approved
direction is valid; do not expand that task to avoid this label.

## Decorative premium simulation

Blur, glow, gradient, texture, or motion substitutes for composition and craft.

## Reference costume

Source objects and styling are copied without translating their function.

## Asset avoidance

Missing product imagery, iconography, or data expression is replaced by more
containers and effects.

## One-layout system

Every screen repeats the same composition regardless of task state.

## Domain-agnostic polish

The direction could be applied to an unrelated product without meaningful
change.

## Signature gimmick

The signature attracts attention but weakens task completion or comprehension.

## Unvalidated premium claim

The claimed level exceeds visual, behavioral, platform, accessibility, or
runtime evidence.

## Category defaults

These are the habits a model reaches for when no decision was made. Each is
allowed only when the brief or the domain earns it:

- same-size cards of icon, heading, and text as the page structure;
- a hero metric (big number, small label, supporting stats) as the opening;
- gradient text, decorative glass or blur, colored side borders thicker than
  1px on cards or alerts, zero-blur offset shadows;
- sparklines, rings, or soft rounded rectangles standing in for content;
- monospace as a "technical" costume instead of for codes, data, or measures;
- emoji or Unicode glyphs in place of the icon system;
- a border and a wide soft shadow on the same element.

## Unstyled browser surfaces

Text selection, caret, scrollbars, focus rings, underline offset, and tabular
numerals ship with browser defaults. Theme them from the product's tokens; they
are the cheapest proof that a screen was built rather than assembled.

## Stop conditions

Return to a simpler direction when real content breaks it, hidden gestures are
required, platform adaptation is cosmetic, implementation cost is
disproportionate, or the discipline revalidation fails.
